Welcome

Mebrahtu F. Weldeghebriel

I am a low-temperature geochemist working at the intersection of ancient-ocean chemistry, isotope geochemistry, and paleoclimatology,and I use that combination to ask how Earth’s surface environments and climate have evolved over geologic time. I work across fluid inclusions in marine halite, carbonates and dolostones, and Antarctic ice, combining in-situ micro-analytical methods (LA-ICP-MS, cryo-SEM-EDS, ICP-QQQ-MS) with mass-balance modeling.

About

I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate in the Department of Geosciences at Princeton University, supported by an NSF Office of Polar Programs Postdoctoral Fellowship and a Gordon and Betty Moore Foundation Postdoctoral Fellowship. My work sits at the intersection of analytical geochemistry, field geology, and Earth-system modeling.

Read more

Four strengths define how I work. I am an analytical geochemist: I build methods for archives most laboratories cannot measure — micrometer-scale fluid inclusions in halite, marine carbonates and dolostones, and Antarctic ice — having developed, installed, and operated LA-ICP-MS, cryo-SEM-EDS, ICP-QQQ-MS, and the column chemistry behind non-traditional stable isotope measurement. I am a field geoscientist: I have mapped evaporites in the Danakil Depression of Eritrea, studied modern halite deposition along the Dead Sea shoreline, and evaluated lithium-bearing basins at more than twenty-five sites across six western U.S. states as an exploration geologist for Lithium Americas Corp. I bring a sedimentological and petrographic foundation, essential for selecting well-preserved samples and reading geochemical data in stratigraphic context. And I build teams: my current projects run through collaborations in seven countries, because no single laboratory holds every archive, isotope system, and field site a question like this requires.

I hold a PhD in Geological Sciences from Binghamton University (2022, advised by Tim Lowenstein) and a BSc in Geology from the Eritrea Institute of Technology (2011). Before Binghamton, I worked as a research assistant on GPS geodesy of the Danakil Block / Afar Rift with MIT and on seismic monitoring of the 2011 Nabro eruption.

Research Program

Five directions built around one capability: quantitative, micrometer-scale chemistry of the archives that record Earth's surface environment. See established work and future directions →

Established

Seawater through deep time

550 Myr of major, minor, trace-element, and lithium-isotope seawater chemistry from fluid inclusions in halite.

Established

Antarctic ice geochronology

An ⁸⁷Sr/⁸⁶Sr chronometer for dating folded, >1-Myr-old blue ice via cryo-cell LA-ICP-MS.

Future direction

Critical minerals in brines

Tracing whether Li-rich basinal brines inherit ancient seawater or form by later basin processes.
