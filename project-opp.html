High-Resolution Chemical Imaging and Dating of Ancient Ice Using LA-ICP-MS

For my NSF Office of Polar Programs Postdoctoral Fellowship at Princeton, I am developing a cryo-cell LA-ICP-MS method to produce two-dimensional impurity maps for layer identification at micrometer resolution, and to measure ⁸⁷Sr/⁸⁶Sr as an independent chronometer in marine salts trapped within the folded and stratigraphically disturbed ice of Antarctic blue-ice areas (1–6 Ma). This complements the NSF Center for Oldest Ice Exploration (COLDEX) goal of recovering a direct atmospheric sample from the mid-Pliocene, the most recent time atmospheric CO₂ was near today's levels.

  • Funding: NSF OPP Postdoctoral Fellowship (Award 2420291, $359,487, 2024–2026)
  • Carried out through the NSF Center for Oldest Ice Exploration (COLDEX)
  • Methods: cryo-cell LA-ICP-MS, ⁸⁷Sr/⁸⁶Sr, micro-analytical mapping
  • Outcomes: 2D impurity maps, age-depth models, calibration datasets
